02 The rule that decides everything

The boundary

The language model decides what the patient said. It never decides how worried to be. It extracts symptoms into plain true/false fields; deterministic rules derived from the ASTCT consensus criteria pick the tier. If you ever see the model returning a tier or a recommendation, that's a bug — and it's the whole pitch.

And two clinical traps. A strong engineer without your background gets both wrong, and neither is obvious from a summary of the criteria.

The antipyretic rule

Once a patient has taken Tylenol or received tocilizumab, fever is no longer required — escalate on blood pressure and oxygen alone. Outpatients medicate at home. A rule that gates on fever would silently under-triage exactly the patients you most need to catch.

Neuro severity is a maximum, not a lookup

Take the worst of several domains — confusion, consciousness, seizure, motor findings — never a single signal. Any reported seizure goes straight to EMERGENT regardless of everything else.

03 What we build, in order

Every tier is independently demoable. Build in order and stop wherever 4pm finds you — Tier 1 finished and clean beats Tier 3 half-broken.

Tier 1 Symptoms in, triage out by 12:30

Five synthetic patients, days 3–12 post-infusion, each with a Q‑Immune pre-infusion risk tier. The deterministic triage() function with its tests. A clinician board showing the cohort by urgency. This alone is a complete demo.

Tier 1b Simulated vitals stream +20 min

A scripted time series of temperature, heart rate, BP and SpO₂ per patient. Labelled "simulated" on screen. No devices, no file parsing — this is what the agent reacts to.

Tier 2 The voice check-in by 15:00

Deepgram transcribes the patient describing how they feel. The model pulls out symptoms as plain true/false fields. Then triage() decides. Show all three side by side — the audit trail is the demo.

Tier 3 The agent decides when to ask by 16:00

A Medplum Bot re-runs triage on every new reading and raises a flag. Then the money feature: overnight vitals drift makes the agent start a check-in on its own. Plus an AI-drafted handover note for the nurse — written after the decision, never part of it.

Stretch Only if Tier 3 is done and committed not before 15:30

Real Apple Watch data · speech latency as a neuro signal · the structured ICE fallback. Don't start any of these if anything above is shaky.

05 The demo

Two minutes. The arc is: vitals drift, the agent asks, the criteria decide, a clinician gets paged.

Open — no screen yet

CAR-T cures people. It also puts them in a hospital bed for two weeks, because of two toxicities we know exactly how to grade — we just can't watch for them at home.

Play the voice check-in

This is Maria, day six after infusion, at home. Every morning Sentinel calls and asks how she's doing. [she says she slept fine, feels okay] Nothing to act on.

Cohort board — all five green

Her nurse sees this.

Vitals chart

Now it's 2am on day seven. Maria is asleep. Her temperature and heart rate have been climbing for six hours. Not a diagnosis on its own — but enough to know something is changing. [if asked: simulated today; in production it streams from a wearable or a home cuff]

Agent triggers the check-in — make this big

So Sentinel doesn't wait for morning. It calls her. She says: "I've been a bit shaky, and this morning I couldn't remember my daughter's name." It asks her to take her temperature: 38.4.

Transcript → features → tier, side by side

Watch what happens to that sentence. The model pulls out three things: new tremor, word-finding difficulty, confusion. Those go into deterministic rules — fever plus new neuro symptoms in a patient we flagged high-risk before infusion. That's EMERGENT. [escalation fires, Maria goes red]

Land it

And look at the note it sent — the agent pulled her own words together with her overnight vitals and drafted a two-sentence handover, so the nurse gets context, not just an alarm. But the note didn't fire the alarm. The thresholds did. The model decides what she said. It never decides how worried to be.

Close

That's a patient who gets tocilizumab this afternoon instead of an ICU bed tomorrow. In a published series, catching it that early kept fifteen of thirty-five patients out of the hospital entirely. We're Q-Immune. Our platform reads protein networks in living cells to predict which patients carry the most toxicity risk — before infusion. This is the other half: it takes that prediction and decides how closely to watch them after.

The gotcha

macOS does not record your computer's own audio — microphone only. So play the app's voice out loud through the laptop speakers and let the mic catch it. Don't wear headphones while recording or your most important moment is silent. Test this tonight, not at 16:30.
