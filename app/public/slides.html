Sentinel

The agent that watches CAR-T patients at home — so they don't have to stay in hospital.

The problem
19 days

Median inpatient stay after CAR-T infusion. Not because the therapy fails — because of what might happen next.

Gregory T, et al. Outpatient vs inpatient cilta-cel: systematic review. Cancers 2026. doi:10.3390/cancers18050755
Why

Two toxicities we know
how to recognise.

Cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity. We just can't watch for them at home — so we admit everyone, for weeks, to be sure.

That inpatient requirement is the bottleneck on CAR-T access. It caps how many patients a centre can treat.
The solution

Sentinel watches
from home.

Vitals stream from the patient's home
Temperature and heart rate climbing together
The agent decides to call — it doesn't wait for morning
Deepgram listens to how they describe their symptoms
Triaged against ASTCT consensus criteria
Escalation written into the EHR via Medplum
The design decision

Nothing that guesses
gets to decide.

Voice is how we collect it. Deterministic rules are how we decide.

We don't let a language model decide how worried to be about a cancer patient. The thresholds come from the published consensus criteria, they're unit-tested, and every escalation cites the exact symptoms and readings behind it.
Why it works
15/35 patients kept out of hospital entirely when grade 1 CRS was caught early. This is the thing Sentinel automates.
$40k saved per patient in the first 30 days outpatient. $53k at 90 days.
4–6 days median stay outpatient, against 19 inpatient. You don't eliminate the admission — you shorten it.

Why Q-Immune

We close the loop.

Predict — QMI reads protein networks to flag toxicity risk before infusion
Tune — that risk sets how sensitively Sentinel watches
Catch — the complication, at home, early
Feed — every event caught is labelled ground truth for the model
The monitoring product generates the training data the prediction product needs. Nobody else has both ends.
